Surrealism emerged as a literary and visual movement that sought to bridge the gap between dreams and reality. It was a direct response to the trauma of WWI, seeking a "higher reality" through the subconscious.

### Core Principles

- **The Unconscious Mind:** Heavily influenced by Sigmund Freud’s psychoanalysis, Surrealists used art to unlock repressed desires and fears.
    
- **Juxtaposition:** Placing two unrelated objects together (e.g., a melting clock on a beach) to create a "shaking" of the viewer's logic.
    

### Key Techniques

- **Automatism:** Creating art without conscious thought or planning, allowing the hand to move randomly to reveal the inner self.
    
- **Veristic Surrealism:** Painting illogical, dream-like scenes with "academic" precision to make the impossible feel disturbingly real (e.g., Salvador Dalí).
    
- **Frottage/Grattage:** Techniques involving rubbing or scraping to create textures that suggest organic, unplanned forms.
