## 2. Ethics: Frameworks for Moral Agency

Ethics examines how we ought to live and how we should treat others. It is divided into three levels: Meta-ethics (the nature of moral claims), Normative Ethics (the rules for behavior), and Applied Ethics (real-world issues).

### Deontology (Duty-Based Ethics)

Championed by **Immanuel Kant**, this framework argues that morality is based on universal duties and rules.

- **The Categorical Imperative:** Act only according to that maxim whereby you can, at the same time, will that it should become a universal law.
    
- **Key Principle:** The "Ends" never justify the "Means." Killing one person to save five is always wrong because it violates the rule "do not kill."
    

### Utilitarianism (Consequentialism)

Founded by **Jeremy Bentham** and refined by **John Stuart Mill**, this theory focuses on the outcomes of actions.

- **The Greatest Happiness Principle:** The morally right action is the one that produces the "greatest good for the greatest number."
    
- **Key Principle:** Morality is a calculation of pleasure versus pain. If sacrificing one person saves five, the utilitarian would argue it is the moral choice.
    

### Virtue Ethics (Character-Based Ethics)

Tracing back to **Aristotle**, this approach focuses on the inherent character of a person rather than specific rules or consequences.

- **The Golden Mean:** Virtue is the balance between two extremes (vices). For example, "Courage" is the mean between "Cowardice" (deficiency) and "Recklessness" (excess).
    
- **Eudaimonia:** The ultimate goal of human life—often translated as "flourishing" or "living well."
    
References
- **Aristotle, _Nicomachean Ethics_:** The foundation of Virtue Ethics, focusing on the "Golden Mean" and the concept of _Eudaimonia_.
    
- **Immanuel Kant, _Groundwork of the Metaphysics of Morals_ (1785):** The definitive text for Deontology and the introduction of the Categorical Imperative.
    
- **John Stuart Mill, _Utilitarianism_ (1861):** The most famous defense of the "Greatest Happiness Principle" and qualitative pleasure.
- - **Philippa Foot, "The Problem of Abortion and the Doctrine of the Double Effect" (1967):** This paper is famous for introducing the **Trolley Problem**, a staple of modern ethical debate.
    
- **Elizabeth Anscombe, "Modern Moral Philosophy" (1958):** A pivotal essay that credited with the 20th-century revival of Virtue Ethics.
    
- **Peter Singer, "Famine, Affluence, and Morality" (1972):** A foundational text for modern "Effective Altruism," challenging our duties to global poverty.
