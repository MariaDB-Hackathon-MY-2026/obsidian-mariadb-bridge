Impressionism was a radical break from the "Academy" style. Instead of painting historical or mythological scenes with invisible brushwork, Impressionists focused on the fleeting effects of light and the modern world.

### Core Principles

- **En Plein Air:** Artists moved out of the studio and into the outdoors to capture the shifting qualities of sunlight. This was made possible by the invention of **portable tin paint tubes**.
    
- **Optical Mixing:** Rather than blending colors on a palette, artists applied short, "broken" brushstrokes of pure color side-by-side, allowing the viewer's eye to mix them at a distance.
    
- **Subject Matter:** They rejected "high art" themes, choosing instead to paint Parisian cafés, railway stations, and suburban landscapes.
    

### Key Characteristics

- **Shadows in Color:** Impressionists realized that shadows are not black or gray, but blue or violet reflections of the sky and surroundings.
    
- **Asymmetrical Composition:** Heavily influenced by the influx of **Japonisme** (Japanese woodblock prints), artists began using "cropped" edges and off-center focal points to mimic a candid snapshot.
