The High Renaissance represents the pinnacle of artistic achievement in Italy, characterized by technical mastery, classical influence, and the elevation of the artist's status from craftsman to genius.

### Core Principles

- **Naturalism & Anatomy:** Artists moved beyond medieval stylization to achieve scientific accuracy. This was fueled by the study of human dissection and the use of **Chiaroscuro** (the treatment of light and shade) to create three-dimensional volume.
    
- **Linear Perspective:** The perfection of the "vanishing point" allowed artists to create a convincing window into a deep, architectural space.
    
- **The "Pyramidal Composition":** A structural technique where the main subjects are arranged to form a triangle, providing a sense of stability and divine order.
    

### Key Techniques

- **Sfumato:** Popularized by Leonardo da Vinci, this involves the blurring of edges and colors to create a "smoky" transition, mimicking how the human eye perceives depth.
    
- **Fresco:** The art of painting on wet lime plaster, requiring immense speed and precision, most famously used in the Vatican’s Stanze and the Sistine Chapel.
    

### Cultural Context

The movement was heavily funded by the Papacy and wealthy merchant families like the Medici. It coincided with **Humanism**, a philosophical shift that emphasized human potential and a revival of Greco-Roman ideals.
