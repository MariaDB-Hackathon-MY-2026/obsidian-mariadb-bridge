
## 1. Epistemology: The Architecture of Belief

Epistemology is the study of knowledge's origin, nature, and limits. It seeks to define the boundary between "knowing" something and merely "believing" it.

### The Standard Definition of Knowledge

Since Plato, knowledge has been traditionally defined as **Justified True Belief (JTB)**. For a subject to know a proposition:

1. **Truth:** The proposition must be true.
    
2. **Belief:** The subject must believe the proposition.
    
3. **Justification:** The subject must have a valid reason for the belief.
    

_Note: This definition was famously challenged by Edmund Gettier, who showed that one can have a justified true belief that is only true by luck (Gettier Problems)._

### Rationalism vs. Empiricism

This is the central debate regarding where our knowledge comes from:

- **Rationalism (René Descartes, Spinoza):** Argues that reason is the primary source of knowledge. Rationalists believe in **innate ideas**—concepts we are born with (like logic or mathematics) that don't require sensory input.
    
- **Empiricism (John Locke, David Hume):** Claims the mind is a _tabula rasa_ (blank slate) at birth. All knowledge is derived from sensory experience. If you can't see, touch, or measure it, it cannot be known.
    

### The Problem of Skepticism

Skepticism questions whether we can know _anything_ for certain.

- **Global Skepticism:** The idea that the external world might be an illusion.
    
- **Descartes’ Methodological Doubt:** To find a foundation for certainty, Descartes doubted everything until he reached "Cogito, ergo sum" (_I think, therefore I am_). Even if a "malicious demon" is tricking his senses, the fact that he is thinking proves his existence.
    
References
- **Plato, _Theaetetus_:** The origin of the "Justified True Belief" (JTB) definition of knowledge and a deep dive into the nature of perception.
    
- **René Descartes, _Meditations on First Philosophy_ (1641):** The cornerstone of Rationalism and the birthplace of "Methodological Doubt" and the _Cogito_.
    
- **John Locke, _An Essay Concerning Human Understanding_ (1689):** The definitive argument for Empiricism and the concept of the _tabula rasa_.
    
- **David Hume, _An Enquiry Concerning Human Understanding_ (1748):** An expansion of Empiricism that introduces the "Problem of Induction" and a skeptical look at cause and effect.
- **Edmund Gettier, "Is Justified True Belief Knowledge?" (1963):** A three-page paper that revolutionized modern epistemology by debunking the JTB model.
    
- **Linda Zagzebski, _On Epistemology_ (2008):** A highly readable modern overview of the field that bridges historical and contemporary debates.
